# Commit-then-reveal, not immediate feedback

Alex taps his entire line (red → colour → red → colour …) with **no feedback**, then
hits Done, and only then does the Reveal show the Coached line, where he matched or took
a valid alternative, and the Divergence. The app never buzzes on a "wrong" tap
mid-sequence.

## Why this is worth recording

The obvious choice for a 10-year-old is immediate feedback — gentler, more game-like. We
deliberately rejected it.

- Immediate feedback turns the exercise into *hunt-the-highlighted-ball*: trial-and-error
  until it lights green. That trains the exact see-ball-pot-ball reflex we are trying to
  remove.
- Committing the whole line first forces the target behaviour: survey the table, plan the
  sequence, *then* execute. It also mirrors reality — at the table nobody tells you shot 3
  was the mistake; you find out when you're stuck.

## Consequences

- A wholly-committed wrong line risks feeling deflating for a young kid. The mitigation is
  the *tone* of the Reveal (teacher, warm, "here's the cleaner line and why"), not a
  switch back to immediate feedback. Paired with the Acceptable-set model, genuine
  alternatives never read as mistakes.
